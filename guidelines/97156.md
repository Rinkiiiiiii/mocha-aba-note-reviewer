# 97156 — Family Adaptive Behavior Treatment Guidance

Source: Council of Autism Service Providers (CASP), Session Note Template Guidance for CPT Code 97156, Version 1.02 (as provided by the practice).

> CASP's own disclaimer, carried forward here: these guidelines should only be used with adequate training in documentation basics, do not guarantee immunity from payer audits or compliance with any individual payer's requirements, and do not contemplate payer-specific documentation rules. Treat a "met" finding here as "meets CASP's general documentation standard," not as a guarantee of payer-specific compliance.

**Descriptor:** Family adaptive behavior treatment guidance, administered by a QHP (with or without the patient present), face-to-face with guardian(s)/caregiver(s), each 15 minutes.

**When 97156 applies:** Can be reported for services delivered to the guardian(s)/caregiver(s) while the client is receiving direct treatment elsewhere (e.g., the client is with a technician in one room while the parent meets with the QHP in another room for family training). 97156 and 97153 are separate, distinct services delivered to different family members by different providers, even when they occur concurrently in the same visit.

## Required elements

1. **Client & provider information**: same fields as 97153/97155.
2. **Session participants**: name(s) of the parent(s)/caregiver(s) present and their relation to the client; whether the client participated or did not participate.
3. **Goals/programs table**: one goal/program per row tied to the treatment plan, the data collection method used per goal, and a summary of the data recorded (same conventions as 97153 — a concise summary is acceptable, and referencing an attached data sheet is fine for high-volume data).
4. **Interventions used to train the caregiver**: from an approved, evidence-based list (Behavior Skills Training, descriptive feedback, modeling, video modeling, or other).
5. **Session summary narrative** — must be objective, relevant to the service, appropriate for a medical record, specific to what occurred, and not duplicative of other sections. Content that belongs here:
   - Events at the start of the session that could affect the outcome (medication change, poor sleep, environment change).
   - Interfering behaviors or environmental factors during the session.
   - If the client was present: the client's response to a behavior-reduction plan, including variation in responding and relevant antecedent/consequent variables.
   - Targets/goals that met mastery criteria during this session.
   - Details of the actual training provided to the caregiver, tied to specific treatment goals — not a generic recitation of ABA principles.
   - The caregiver's report of how the client responds to protocols when the QHP is NOT present (a specific, quoted-style observation is good practice — e.g., "Parent reports the client complies with 'first/then' statements when the QHP is present but protests when the QHP is not present").
   - New caregiver concerns that may require future protocol modification (to be addressed at the next 97155 visit).
   - Relevant client updates that might affect future caregiver-guidance sessions.
   - Caregiver-reported information warranting a referral or a change to care coordination (e.g., a new medication, a new medical concern).
   - If the client was present: how the client responded to the caregiver during the session.
   - Data on treatment fidelity as implemented by the caregiver, and/or interobserver agreement between the caregiver and the QHP.
6. **Signatures**: legible legal name and credentials, printed name/credentials, date signed.

## Common deficiencies to flag

- The narrative is a generic checklist of ABA principles/interventions with no connection to this caregiver's actual performance or this client's actual case.
- No description of the caregiver's observed competency or response to the training — this is required, not optional.
- Missing session-participant information (who was present, relation to the client, whether the client participated).
- The narrative fails to note whether the client was present, or omits the client's response to the caregiver when the client was present.
- Caregiver-reported concerns or updates aren't documented (these matter for coordinating future 97155/97153 care).
- Missing identifying/session information, or missing signature.

## Severity guidance

- **High**: no description of the caregiver's actual training performance/response; missing session-participant info.
- **Medium**: narrative present but generic/duplicative rather than specific to this session.
- **Low**: minor completeness/formatting issues.
