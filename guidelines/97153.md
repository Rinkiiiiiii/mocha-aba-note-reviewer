# 97153 — Adaptive Behavior Treatment by Protocol

Source: Council of Autism Service Providers (CASP), Session Note Template Guidance for CPT Code 97153, Version 1.05 (as provided by the practice).

> CASP's own disclaimer, carried forward here: these guidelines should only be used with adequate training in documentation basics, do not guarantee immunity from payer audits or compliance with any individual payer's requirements, and do not contemplate payer-specific documentation rules. Treat a "met" finding here as "meets CASP's general documentation standard," not as a guarantee of payer-specific compliance.

**Descriptor:** Adaptive behavior treatment by protocol, administered by a technician under the direction of a physician or other qualified health care professional (QHP), face-to-face with one patient, each 15 minutes.

**Coding notes:**
- If no protocols have been modified and a QHP is simply acting in place of the technician, 97153 should be reported with a modifier indicating delivery by a higher-level provider.
- 97153 and 97155 can be reported concurrently if the criteria for both are independently met (e.g., a technician delivers 97153 while a QHP concurrently directs them under 97155). A single QHP may NOT report 97153 and 97155 concurrently for themselves.

## Required elements

1. **Client & provider information**: client legal name (should match the service authorization), client ID or DOB, client diagnosis (ICD-10) establishing medical necessity, organization name (matches authorization), provider name and credentials (matching taxonomy/CAQH/licensure), place of service (matches the authorized POS billing code), modality (in-person vs. HIPAA-compliant telehealth).
2. **Session timing**: date of service, actual start time, actual end time, duration (the true elapsed time, not rounded — referencing a billing record is acceptable).
3. **Goals/programs table**: one goal/program per row tied to the treatment plan (a short-term objective may be listed under its overarching goal), the data collection method used per goal (rate, frequency, latency, duration, momentary time sampling, whole/partial interval, frequency per trial/opportunity/step, ABC data, or other), and a summary of the data recorded per goal. A concise summary (e.g., "7/10") is acceptable in place of every raw data point; referencing an attached data sheet is acceptable when volume is high.
4. **Interventions**: which evidence-based interventions were used during the session, selected from the organization's approved, payer-compliant list. Non-evidence-based interventions must not appear.
5. **Session summary narrative** — must be objective, relevant to the service, appropriate for a medical record, and specific to what occurred during the session. It must NOT simply restate information already captured elsewhere in the note (the goals table, the interventions checklist, etc.). Content that belongs in the narrative includes: events at the start of the session that could affect the outcome (medication change, poor sleep, environment change); interfering behaviors or environmental factors observed during the session; any new independent behavior not already reflected in the data (e.g., an unprompted request, spontaneous imitation); the client's response to a behavior-reduction plan, including variation in responding and relevant antecedent/consequent variables; the client's response to procedures meant to build or strengthen adaptive skills; additional detail related to ABC data; any variation in topography, magnitude, frequency, or duration of a targeted behavior; and anything about treatment targets, programs, or goals that needs QHP attention.
6. **Signatures**: legible legal name and credentials of the delivering provider, printed name/credentials, and the date the note was signed.

## Common deficiencies to flag

- The narrative merely restates the checkbox/table data instead of adding objective clinical information not captured elsewhere.
- The narrative is subjective or vague ("had a good day") rather than objective and specific to what occurred.
- A data collection method isn't identified per goal, or the data summary is missing or too sparse to support medical necessity.
- Interventions listed aren't clearly tied to the specific targeted behaviors/goals they were used for.
- Missing or inconsistent session timing (start/end/duration), or duration that doesn't reconcile with billed units.
- Client, provider, or diagnosis information is incomplete or inconsistent with what an authorization would show.
- A QHP appears to have delivered the session in place of a technician without the required modifier noted.
- Missing signature, credentials, or date.

## Severity guidance

- **High**: missing objective narrative content describing the client's actual response to behavior-reduction/skill-acquisition procedures; missing required identifying or session info (client, provider, date, duration); interventions that aren't evidence-based.
- **Medium**: goals/data table present but the data summary is too thin to support medical necessity; narrative present but largely duplicative of other sections.
- **Low**: minor completeness/formatting issues that don't affect clinical or billing substance (e.g., signature date formatting).
