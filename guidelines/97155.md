# 97155 — Adaptive Behavior Treatment with Protocol Modification

Source: Council of Autism Service Providers (CASP), Session Note Template Guidance for CPT Code 97155, Version 1.02 (as provided by the practice).

> CASP's own disclaimer, carried forward here: these guidelines should only be used with adequate training in documentation basics, do not guarantee immunity from payer audits or compliance with any individual payer's requirements, and do not contemplate payer-specific documentation rules. Treat a "met" finding here as "meets CASP's general documentation standard," not as a guarantee of payer-specific compliance.

**Descriptor:** Adaptive behavior treatment with protocol modification, administered by a QHP — which may include simultaneous direction of a technician — face-to-face with one patient, each 15 minutes.

**When 97155 applies:**
- (A) A QHP conducts 1:1 direct treatment with the client to observe changes in behavior or troubleshoot treatment protocols; or
- (B) A QHP joins the client and a technician during a treatment session to direct the technician in implementing a new or modified protocol — in this case, 97153 should be reported concurrently (by the technician).

## Required elements

1. **Client & provider information**: same fields as 97153 — client name/ID/diagnosis, organization, provider name/credentials, place of service, modality, date, start/end time, duration.
2. **Face-to-face observation/evaluation**: whether the QHP made observations to determine if protocol components were functioning effectively or required adjustment, and the conclusion (effective / no changes needed, vs. adjustments required). The narrative should name the specific programs/protocol components observed, distinguishing which needed no changes from which needed changes (e.g., "Receptive Labeling... no adjustments indicated. Adjustments needed for Observational Learning protocol.") — not just restate that "observations were made."
3. **If adjustments were made**: which specific components were adjusted (treatment targets, treatment goals, observation/measurement, reinforcers, reinforcer delivery, prompts, instruction, materials, discriminative stimuli, contextual variables, or other). The narrative should emphasize WHAT was done and WHY (the clinical rationale/analysis behind the change) rather than only re-listing which checkboxes were selected.
4. **If active technician direction occurred**: the technician's name and credentials, and which direction activities took place (implement-then-observe role swap, correcting implementation errors, modeling correct implementation, training the technician on a modified protocol, feedback/instruction on implementation, independently observing/recording to check interobserver agreement or identify retraining needs, or other). The narrative must explain why this direction was necessary for this client's benefit/progress, not just restate the checklist.
5. **If the QHP directly implemented the protocol with the client**: for the purpose of determining if changes are needed, or testing a modified protocol. The narrative should describe what the QHP did, the client's response, and the clinical implications — testing a protocol may legitimately result in "no change."
6. **"Other" section**, if used: a brief summary of other 97155-appropriate service not captured above (e.g., targets/goals that met mastery criteria during this session).
7. **Signatures**: legible legal name and credentials, printed name/credentials, date signed.

## Common deficiencies to flag

- Checkboxes indicate a protocol modification occurred, but the narrative doesn't explain the clinical rationale for the change — it just restates what was checked.
- No evidence of actual direct observation — the note reads like a data summary rather than describing the QHP's observation/evaluation activity.
- Technician-direction is checked but the narrative doesn't tie the direction back to why this specific client needed it.
- A technician appears to have been actively directed during the encounter but 97153 isn't noted as reported concurrently (worth flagging as a possible billing/coding mismatch — final billing determination remains the provider's responsibility).
- Indications that a single QHP's note bills both 97153 and 97155 concurrently for themselves — not permitted per this guidance (only a technician-delivered 97153 alongside a QHP's 97155 direction qualifies).
- Missing identifying/session information, or missing signature.

## Severity guidance

- **High**: no rationale given for protocol modifications; no documentation of direct observation; an apparent violation of the concurrent-billing rule (single QHP billing both codes).
- **Medium**: narrative present but thin on the "why," or missing detail on technician direction/interobserver agreement.
- **Low**: minor completeness/formatting issues.
